Why this book exists

Sule Abdul Rahman grew up in Bando, near Elmina. He lost his father early. His mother's illness stretched the family thin for years that mattered. What carried him through was not a single lucky break — it was learning, slowly and out of necessity, how to build structure out of very little.

He carried that instinct into agriculture and organisational work — first at CSIR-SARI, then with the Masara N'Arziki Farmers Association, then as Technical/Commercial Business Partner Coordinator at the Agricultural Labour Services Agency — before founding an agribusiness consulting practice in Northern Ghana. Everywhere he has worked, he has seen the same pattern: people working harder than anyone should have to, in organisations that still cannot tell you what a bag of maize actually costs to produce.

From Effort to Systems is what he has learned about closing that gap — written for the people doing the work, not the people funding it.

An excerpt from the introduction

At 5:30 a.m., the cooperative chairman was already in the field. By 8:00 p.m., he was still solving problems. Everyone called him hardworking. Yet his cooperative was losing money.

Development programmes have trained millions of farmers. Yet many communities remain trapped in the same cycle. The uncomfortable reality is that training alone rarely changes organisational performance. Systems do.

In 2021, I visited a farmer group that had received more support in three years than some cooperatives receive in a decade. They had tractors, seed, fertiliser, and training. Yet they could not tell me how much it cost to produce a bag of maize. The problem was not resources. The problem was that nobody had built a management system.

— From Effort to Systems

What's inside

Fourteen chapters and fourteen practitioner workbooks, built for agribusiness organisations across West Africa — not generic frameworks retrofitted with local examples.

Field-tested frameworks

Established management thinking, including Boehlje's agribusiness systems framework, reworked for cooperatives, agriprocessors, and agripreneurs operating with real constraints.

Practitioner workbooks

Fourteen companion workbooks that turn each framework into a working document — something a cooperative can actually fill in and use, not just read.

First-person openers

Each chapter opens with a real career episode, not a hypothetical — including a named mentee's story from the ground.

Written for the practitioner

Not a donor report and not a textbook. Built for the person who has to make the organisation work on Monday morning.
