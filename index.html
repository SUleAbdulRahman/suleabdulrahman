Why this book exists

Sule Abdul Rahman grew up in Bando, near Elmina. He lost his father early. His mother's illness stretched the family thin for years that mattered. What carried him through was not a single lucky break — it was learning, slowly and out of necessity, how to build structure out of very little.

He carried that instinct into agriculture and organisational work — national service at CSIR-SARI in Nyankpala, six years as a Technical Field Specialist with the Masara N'Arziki Farmers Association, then three years as Commercial Business Partner Coordinator with the Agricultural Labour Services Agency — before founding an agribusiness consulting practice in Northern Ghana. Everywhere he has worked, he has seen the same pattern: people working harder than anyone should have to, in organisations that still cannot tell you what a bag of maize actually costs to produce.

From Effort to Systems is what he has learned about closing that gap — written for the people doing the work, not the people funding it.

An excerpt from the introduction

I did not set out to write a book about systems. I set out to understand why agribusinesses keep failing, even when the people running them are working harder than anyone I have ever met.

I watched more than half of a 1,026.5-tonne onion harvest (41,060 bags of 25 kg) rot or sell at a loss because we had built a production system but not a marketing system. I sat across the table from farmer groups that had received more support in three years than most cooperatives receive in a decade (tractors, seed, fertiliser and training), yet they could not tell me what it cost to produce a single bag of maize.

I spent seven years building a rubber plantation, contracted a trusted family member to manage it, received incomplete records, and eventually came back from the United Kingdom to find the farm had effectively been abandoned. I lost somewhere between GH₵300,000 and GH₵400,000 because I built a crop but not a business.

— From Effort to Systems

Praise for From Effort to Systems

What's inside

Fourteen chapters and fourteen practitioner workbooks, built for agribusiness organisations across West Africa — not generic frameworks retrofitted with local examples.

Field-tested frameworks

Established management thinking, including Michael Boehlje's work on structural change and strategic uncertainty in agribusiness, reworked for cooperatives, agriprocessors, and agripreneurs operating with real constraints.

Practitioner workbooks

Fourteen companion workbooks that turn each framework into a working document — something a cooperative can actually fill in and use, not just read. Book owners get the complete A4 collection free from Bando Books.

From My Field Notebook

Each chapter opens with a real career episode from the field, not a hypothetical — including the story of a former mentee, Isaac.

Written for the practitioner

Not a donor report and not a textbook. Built for the person who has to make the organisation work on Monday morning.
